## Understanding what Monolithic Lab actually does

### Is Monolithic Lab consulting?

Yes and no.

There is a diagnostic, framing, and decision-making dimension to the work. But Monolithic Lab is not designed as a firm that hands over recommendations and disappears. The work can extend into structuring, installing systems, changing how things run, and building what is missing when that is what the situation requires.

### Is Monolithic Lab a fractional CTO service?

That is one of the most natural formats, but not the only one.

Monolithic Lab also works on:
- operating structure diagnostics;
- engineering organization and process issues;
- systems, internal tools, and integrations;
- practical AI workflows for internal operations;
- short interventions around a critical issue.

### Does Monolithic Lab build things too, or only advise?

It can do both. Some situations require judgment, framing, and better decisions. Others require an actual system, integration, workflow, or operating layer to be put in place.

The rule is simple: only build what creates real leverage.

## What kind of companies are a good fit?

### What does a good fit look like?

A good fit is usually a growing or transitional company starting to feel that too much of how it operates is still implicit, fragile, or unnecessarily costly.

Typically:
- startups and scale-ups;
- SMBs with a real operating structure issue;
- teams ranging from around ten to a few dozen people;
- contexts where the problem is organizational and technical at the same time.

### When is Monolithic Lab not the right option?

If the need is purely execution with no structuring dimension, a specialized freelancer may be a better fit.

If the company needs a full delivery team, an agency or larger services firm is probably more appropriate.

If leadership does not want to question how the company currently works or delegate a real mandate, the intervention is unlikely to create enough value.

## What does a first conversation look like?

The first conversation is there to understand the situation, not to force a sale.

The goal is to identify:
- what is actually blocking the company;
- why it matters now;
- what kind of intervention would help;
- whether Monolithic Lab is the right fit.

If it is not, it is better to say so early.

## Cadence, duration, and formats

### What is the usual engagement cadence?

That depends on the format:
- **fractional technical leadership**: typically 1 to 3 days per week;
- **diagnosis / operating structure**: concentrated work over a few weeks;
- **focused sprint**: a short, dense intervention around a specific issue;
- **ongoing advisory / support**: regular involvement based on how much continuity is actually useful.

### On-site or remote?

Both. The right balance depends on the context. Some of the work can be done remotely, but certain phases benefit a lot from direct exposure to the team and the operating reality on the ground.

### Is there a minimum commitment?

There is no dogma here. What matters is choosing a format that matches the problem. Some interventions are short and tightly scoped, others need continuity.

## What happens at the end?

### What happens when the engagement ends?

The engagement should leave the company with more clarity and less dependency.

That can mean:
- documentation;
- explicit decisions;
- a more autonomous team;
- systems that are easier to own;
- a clean transition to an internal CTO or a stabilized team.

### What if we hire a full-time CTO during the engagement?

That is often a good outcome. Monolithic Lab can help prepare the transition, clarify the role, and transfer whatever needs to be transferred.

## Pricing and engagement model

### How does pricing work?

It depends on the type of intervention, but the logic stays simple and explicit. Pricing is discussed once the actual scope is understood.

### Why not publish one universal rate?

Because a short diagnostic, a fractional CTO engagement, and a focused structuring sprint are not the same type of work. A clear proposal tailored to the real need is more useful than a fake universal price.

## Confidentiality and independence

### Is an NDA possible?

Yes. As soon as the conversation involves sensitive information, a confidentiality agreement can be put in place.

### Is Monolithic Lab tied to vendors, integrators, or partners that need to be pushed?

No. Independence is part of the model. Recommendations are supposed to serve the client's situation, not a commercial relationship.

### Can Monolithic Lab work with my existing team?

Yes. That is often where the work is most useful: giving the team more structure, clearer responsibilities, and better systems without replacing the people already in place.

## AI and systems

### Does Monolithic Lab "do AI"?

Yes, but not as empty positioning.

The question is not how to add AI everywhere. The question is where it improves execution in a concrete way: documentation, research, internal workflows, targeted assistants, processing chains, operational memory.

### How do you avoid gimmicky AI work?

By starting from the real problem rather than from the available technology. If AI does not create concrete leverage, it is better not to use it.
