## Definition

An **interim CTO** is a technology executive hired for a set period to fill an empty or failing CTO seat. It’s interim management applied to technical leadership.

You’ll also hear transitional CTO.

The engagement has three defining traits:

- **a fast start**: they step in within days, not months;
- **a clear goal**: steady the team, make the calls that can’t wait, prepare what comes next;
- **a planned end**: they leave once the successor is hired and settled in.

## Interim CTO or fractional CTO?

Both hold the full CTO role. What sets them apart is the starting point.

A [fractional CTO](/en-us/articles/what-is-a-fractional-cto) covers a need that is **ongoing but partial**: the company needs a CTO, just not five days a week. The engagement can last more than a year.

An interim CTO covers a **break**: the role existed, and it’s now empty or not working. The time commitment is heavier, often close to full-time at first, and the engagement is shorter.

The two sometimes follow each other: an interim engagement turns fractional while the company decides whether it really needs a full-time CTO.

## Typical situations

**The CTO leaves.** A resignation, a negotiated exit, a disagreement with leadership. Hiring a successor takes months. Meanwhile, the team has no anchor, and pending decisions pile up.

**The CTO has outgrown the role, or the role has outgrown them.** The founding CTO built the product, but the company has changed size. The interim CTO holds the role while the company reorganizes technical leadership, sometimes by moving the founder into a role that suits them better.

**A long absence.** Parental leave, illness, unavailability. The company needs continuity without permanently replacing the person.

**A critical event.** An acquisition, a merger, due diligence, a major incident, an urgent migration. The situation calls for experienced technical leadership right now.

## How an engagement runs

### Take control

Meet every member of the team. Inventory projects in flight, commitments to customers, and risks. Check who holds critical access, where the backups are, which contracts are up for renewal. Unblock stalled decisions.

### Steady the team and lead

Restore clear priorities and a delivery rhythm. Address the risks identified. Start the AI transformation instead of postponing it: a transition is often the right moment to change how the team works. Keep the team: an empty CTO seat often sets off a chain of departures, and each one makes recovery longer. Tell leadership, and the board if needed, where the technology really stands.

### Prepare the succession

The interim CTO knows the job from the inside. That makes them the best person to define the successor’s profile, join the interviews, and assess candidates against the real situation rather than a job description.

### Hand over

Document the architecture, decisions, and open projects. Support the new CTO’s first weeks. Leave.

## How long?

Long enough to steady things, then hire and onboard the successor: usually a few months. An interim engagement that drags on with no search underway is often a sign the company actually needs a [fractional CTO](/en-us/fractional-cto).

## How to choose one

- **They’ve held the role before.** An interim engagement is no time to learn the job.
- **They understand systems they didn’t build, fast.** It’s the first skill of an interim CTO, and it comes from experience.
- **They write things down.** A written assessment, documented decisions, a planned handover.
- **They have no reason to stay.** No engagement to stretch out, no candidate to place for a fee.
- **They can talk to both audiences.** Reassuring a worried team and giving a board a candid picture take different conversations.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Waiting.** Every week without technical leadership adds to the pile of stalled decisions and departures.
- **A vague mandate.** From day one, the team has to know the interim CTO makes the calls.
- **Hiring the successor without them.** They know the open role better than anyone.
- **Forgetting the exit.** The end date and success criteria are set at the start.

## Bottom line

An interim CTO keeps an empty seat from turning into a crisis. They take over technical leadership, steady the team, and turn hiring the successor into an informed decision.
