## Definition

A **fixed-price project** answers a bounded question: how sound is our technology, where can AI help us, does this idea work, can we put it in front of real users. Scope, deliverable, timeline, and price are written down before work starts, and don’t change along the way without both sides agreeing.

A fixed-price project isn’t a CTO role. It doesn’t lead the team or make the day-to-day calls: it delivers a result, then ends. If you need someone to lead technology over time, that’s a [fractional CTO](/en-us/fractional-cto) or [interim CTO](/en-us/interim-cto) role.

## Four formats

### Technology assessment

**The question**: how sound is our technology, and where are the risks?

An independent assessment of architecture, code, team, security, and costs, in 2 to 8 weeks depending on scope.

**When**: before a fundraise or a sale, before hiring a CTO, after a serious incident, or for a second opinion on a team or a vendor.

**Deliverable**: a written report, risks ranked by severity, a prioritized action plan, a debrief with leadership.

### AI exploration

**The question**: where can AI really help our company?

Interviews and observation in the teams concerned, an inventory of use cases, first tests on your data. The exploration starts from the teams’ real work, not from a tool catalog.

**When**: AI is on the leadership agenda, a few people use it on their own, and no one knows what it would really bring.

**Deliverable**: use cases ranked by gain, risk, and feasibility, and an adoption plan built with the teams.

### POC

**The question**: does this idea work on our data?

A prototype on your real data, tested by the people who will use it, with a success criterion set at the start. A POC that fails fast is a good outcome: it costs less than a project that fails slowly.

**When**: an idea looks promising, but the investment needed to build it for real calls for proof.

**Deliverable**: a working prototype, measured results, a clear go or no-go recommendation.

### MVP

**The question**: can we put a first version into production?

A first version used by real users, on a deliberately narrow scope: an internal product, a business tool, an AI feature, or a new service. Built with AI agents to move fast, reviewed and tested to hold up in production.

**When**: the idea has proven itself, and the company doesn’t have the team, or the bandwidth, to build it.

**Deliverable**: a product in production, the code, the documentation, and a team able to evolve it.

## How a project runs

<step-timeline>
  <step-item number="01" title="Frame the question">A first conversation to understand the situation and state the question the project has to answer.</step-item>
  <step-item number="02" title="Write the scope">A written proposal: what’s included, what isn’t, the deliverable, the timeline, a firm price.</step-item>
  <step-item number="03" title="Work on the real thing">On site and with your teams, on your systems and your data, with regular progress updates.</step-item>
  <step-item number="04" title="Deliver">The agreed deliverable, on the agreed date, presented to leadership and the teams concerned.</step-item>
  <step-item number="05" title="Hand over">Documentation, handover to the team, and a candid recommendation on what comes next, even when that’s nothing.</step-item>
</step-timeline>

## Fixed-price or CTO role?

A fixed-price project fits when the question is specific and the answer fits in a deliverable. If you need someone to decide, lead the team, and drive the AI transformation over time, that’s a CTO role.

The two sometimes follow each other: an assessment shows technical leadership is missing, an AI exploration calls for someone to drive adoption. A fixed-price project can come before a CTO role, but never requires one.

## The rules

- one senior point of contact, from the first call to the handover
- a written scope: what’s included, what isn’t
- a firm price, with no overruns
- deliverables your team can take over
- no commissions from software vendors, integrators, or AI providers
